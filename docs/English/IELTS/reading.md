# Reading

## Academic Reading format

The IELTS Academic Reading test has **60 minutes**, **3 sections**, and **40 questions**. The texts are written for a non-specialist audience and may include different styles, arguments, diagrams, graphs, or illustrations. Read the official [IELTS Academic Reading test format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-reading) for current test details.

## General routine

1. Read the task instructions, including whether options or paragraphs can be reused and the word limit.
2. Underline key words in the question and anticipate paraphrases; scan for likely locations.
3. Read the relevant sentences in context and confirm the evidence supports the answer.
4. Check that the answer addresses exactly what is asked, follows the required format, and stays within the word limit.
5. Use only the passage. Distinguish a contradiction from information the passage simply does not provide.

## Question types

### 1. Multiple Choice

**Approach:** Identify the question's focus, locate the relevant passage, and compare each option against the full meaning of the evidence. For multi-select questions, check how many answers are required.

**Common traps:** Distractors may repeat passage words but change the meaning, use an unsupported detail, or be only partly correct. Do not choose by keyword match alone.

### 2. True / False / Not Given

**Approach:** Match each statement to factual information in the passage. Mark **False** only when the passage contradicts it; mark **Not Given** when the passage neither confirms nor contradicts it.

**Common traps:** Treating a related detail as proof, or using outside knowledge. A missing detail is not automatically False.

### 3. Yes / No / Not Given

**Approach:** Check whether each statement agrees with the **writer's opinion or claim**. **No** means the writer contradicts it; **Not Given** means the writer's view is not stated clearly enough to agree or disagree.

**Common traps:** Confusing this task with factual True / False / Not Given, or treating a reported opinion as the writer's own. Do not use personal knowledge.

### 4. Matching Information

**Approach:** Scan paragraphs for the specific detail requested, such as an example, reason, comparison, or explanation. Confirm the match in context.

**Common traps:** Matching the paragraph's general topic rather than the requested detail. A paragraph may contain several relevant ideas. Check the instructions: some tasks allow a paragraph to be used more than once, others do not.

### 5. Matching Headings

**Approach:** Find the **main idea** of each paragraph. Summarize its overall point in a few words, then choose the heading that covers the paragraph as a whole.

**Common traps:** Choosing a heading based on one striking example or supporting detail. Headings are not normally reused; follow the task instructions and expect unused options.

### 6. Matching Features

**Approach:** Identify what each option represents (for example, a person, place, or theory), then locate the statement's evidence and match the relationship accurately.

**Common traps:** Mixing up similar names or assigning a statement to the person it discusses rather than the person who made it. Some options may be unused or reusable: check the instructions.

### 7. Matching Sentence Endings

**Approach:** Understand the sentence beginning, locate its idea in the passage, and test endings for both meaning and grammar. Eliminate endings that add unsupported information.

**Common traps:** Selecting a plausible ending that does not match the passage, or choosing by grammar alone. The list may contain extra endings; check whether reuse is allowed.

### 8. Sentence Completion

**Approach:** Predict the kind of word needed, find the corresponding passage detail, and copy the required word(s) accurately. Check grammar and meaning in the completed sentence.

**Common traps:** Exceeding the stated word limit, changing a required form, or copying nearby words that do not answer the gap. Follow the exact limit in the instructions.

### 9. Summary / Note / Table / Flow-chart Completion

**Approach:** Use headings, sequence, and key terms to find the relevant part of the passage. Read around each gap, predict the required information and word type, then complete the summary, notes, table, or steps. Answers may not follow passage order.

**Common traps:** Assuming the summary covers the whole text or that gaps follow passage order. Check whether answers must come from the text or a supplied list, and obey the word limit.

### 10. Diagram Label Completion

**Approach:** Relate each label to the diagram's position or part, then find the passage description that identifies it. Check that the answer fits the label and the stated word limit.

**Common traps:** Confusing nearby parts or assuming labels follow passage order. Use the passage evidence and exact instructions; do not add explanatory words that exceed the limit.

### 11. Short-answer Questions

**Approach:** Identify the requested fact (who, what, where, when, why, or how much), locate it in the passage, and answer directly with the necessary words or number.

**Common traps:** Giving an incomplete answer, adding unnecessary explanation, or exceeding the word limit. Use words from the passage where required and make sure the response answers the exact question.

## Quick distinction

- **Matching Headings** asks for a paragraph's main idea; **Matching Information** asks where a specific detail appears.
- Never assume paragraphs or answer options can be repeated. Some tasks permit reuse and others do not, so follow the instructions for that task.
- For completion, diagram-label, and short-answer tasks, follow the word limit and check that the answer is complete enough to answer exactly what is asked.
